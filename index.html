Lesson 1 · 45 minutes

Inside the Digital World

What can you touch? What makes the computer do things?

Hardware Software Applications

1 · Look and discuss 8 min

Hardware

Physical parts of a computer or digital device — things you can touch.

Keyboard · mouse · monitor · microphone

Software

Programs and instructions that help the computer do useful things.

Browser · Scratch · word processor · drawing app
Show a real classroom laptop. Ask pupils to name parts they can touch and programs they use. Explain that a touchscreen is hardware even though software appears on it.

2 · Hardware or software? 10 min

Choose a category for each item. You can change your mind and discuss your reasoning.

Hardware: headphones, webcam, touchscreen, USB memory stick. Software: Scratch, PowerPoint, web browser, drawing application.

3 · Choose the right tool 12 min

For each task, choose a suitable type of software. More than one answer may make sense.

Suggested types: word processor, presentation app, spreadsheet, drawing app. Invite pupils to explain their choices rather than memorise a particular brand.

4 · Digital detective 10 min

Look at your classroom computer. Find three pieces of hardware and three software applications.

5 · Exit question 5 min

How are hardware and software different?

Lesson 2 · 45 minutes

Files, Folders and Fixing Problems

Learn how to keep your digital work organised — and what to do when things go wrong.

1 · File or folder? 10 min

Folder

Holds and organises files.

File

A saved item such as a document, picture or presentation.

Demonstrate creating a folder named ICT Practice, saving a document inside, closing it and reopening it. Use the school's actual file manager.

2 · File organisation challenge 15 min

Complete these steps on your own computer. Tick each step when you've done it.

0 of 6 steps completed

3 · Oops! Something went wrong 12 min

Undo: Ctrl+Z on Windows, Command+Z on Mac. For a missing file: check folder, search filename or Recent. If an app freezes, wait and ask an adult before forcing it to close. Do not deliberately crash pupil devices.

4 · Name that file 5 min

Which filename would be easiest to recognise next week for a rainforest research project?

Why did you choose it?

5 · Exit check 3 min

How could you undo a mistake? What would you try if you couldn't find a saved file?

Lesson 3 · 45 minutes

Smart Searching and Using Images

Find useful information, check it carefully, and make responsible choices about pictures.

1 · Compare searches 8 min

Very broad search

animals

Lots of results, but not necessarily what you need.

Specific search

how do penguins stay warm in Antarctica

More focused on the question you want answered.

Discuss keywords and why a specific search can help. Explain that even convincing search results may be wrong or incomplete.

2 · Improve the search 10 min

Write a better search phrase for each research question.

3 · The third-source challenge 12 min

Two websites disagree about an animal's lifespan. What would you do next?

A third source can help — but only if it is trustworthy. Think about who published the information, whether it is relevant, and whether other reliable sources agree.

Now investigate one simple fact using two sources approved by your teacher.

4 · Can I use this picture? 10 min

Different images come with different responsibilities. Discuss each situation.

Key point: educational use does not automatically permit unrestricted copying. Check licence or permission and give credit when required. Attribution by itself does not always grant permission.

5 · Exit ticket 5 min

Write one thing you've learned about searching, checking information or using images.
